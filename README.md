# Credit Scoring Business Understanding

The Basel II Accord places strong emphasis on accurate risk measurement, transparency, and regulatory accountability, which directly influences the choice of credit scoring models. Under Basel II, financial institutions must be able to clearly explain how risk estimates are produced and how they relate to capital adequacy decisions. This makes interpretability and thorough documentation essential, as models are subject to regulatory review, internal audits, and business validation. An interpretable model helps ensure that risk drivers are understandable to both technical and non-technical stakeholders, reduces model risk, and supports compliance with supervisory requirements.

In the absence of a direct “default” label, creating a proxy variable becomes necessary to approximate borrower default behavior using available data, such as missed payments or severe delinquency. This proxy enables model development and risk differentiation when true default outcomes are not recorded. However, relying on a proxy introduces business risks, as the proxy may not perfectly reflect actual default events. Misalignment between the proxy and true credit risk can lead to biased predictions, incorrect risk pricing, suboptimal credit decisions, and potential regulatory concerns if the proxy is not well-justified and validated.

There is a fundamental trade-off between simple, interpretable models and complex, high-performance models in a regulated financial environment. Models such as Logistic Regression with Weight of Evidence offer transparency, stability, and ease of explanation, which are highly valued by regulators and risk managers. In contrast, complex models like Gradient Boosting often deliver superior predictive performance but operate as black boxes, making them harder to interpret, justify, and govern. In practice, financial institutions must balance predictive accuracy with interpretability, often favoring models that provide sufficient performance while remaining explainable, auditable, and aligned with regulatory expectations.
